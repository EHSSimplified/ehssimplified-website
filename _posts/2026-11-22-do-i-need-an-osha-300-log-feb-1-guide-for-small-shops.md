If your company had more than 10 employees at any time in 2026, you almost certainly need an OSHA 300 log. You must post the year-end summary, Form 300A, from February 1 to April 30, 2027. If your plant had 20 to 249 employees, you must also submit that summary to OSHA online by March 2, 2027.

Here's what that means for a small manufacturer, in plain language.

## Do I need an OSHA 300 log if I have 30 employees?

Yes, in most cases. The test is your peak headcount across the whole company last year ([29 CFR 1904.1](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1904/1904.1)):

- **10 or fewer employees at all times** — you don't have to keep the log.
- **More than 10 at any time** — you keep the log, unless your industry is on OSHA's partially exempt list. Check it before you assume.

Size never exempts you from reporting a serious injury. Report a fatality within 8 hours. Report an in-patient hospitalization, amputation or loss of an eye within 24 hours ([29 CFR 1904.39](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1904/1904.39)).

## What counts as a recordable injury?

Record a work-related injury or illness if it leads to any of these ([29 CFR 1904.7](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1904/1904.7)):

- death
- days away from work
- restricted work or a job transfer
- medical treatment beyond first aid
- loss of consciousness
- a significant diagnosed injury, such as a fractured bone or punctured eardrum

### First aid or medical treatment?

This line settles most questions. A bandage, an over-the-counter pain reliever at normal strength, or a tetanus shot is first aid. Stitches, staples or a rigid splint is medical treatment, and the case goes on the log.

## What do I have to do by February 1?

Work through this list in January:

- **Step 1: Close out the 2026 log.** Compare it with your injury reports, first-aid log and workers' comp claims. Add any case that's missing.
- **Step 2: Fill in Form 300A.** Total the columns. Add your average number of employees and total hours worked. No recordable cases? Enter zeros. You still post it.
- **Step 3: Get the right signature.** A company executive must certify it: an owner of a sole proprietorship or partnership, a corporate officer, the highest-ranking official at the site, or that person's direct supervisor ([29 CFR 1904.32](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1904/1904.32)). A safety coordinator who isn't one of these can't sign it.
- **Step 4: Post it by February 1.** Put it where you post other employee notices. Leave it up until April 30.
- **Step 5: Submit online by March 2.** Manufacturing (NAICS 31-33) is on OSHA's list of designated industries ([Appendix A](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1904/1904SubpartEAppA)). An establishment with 20 to 249 employees submits its 300A through OSHA's [Injury Tracking Application](https://www.osha.gov/injuryreporting). So does any establishment with 250 or more. Part-time, seasonal and temporary workers count toward the total ([29 CFR 1904.41](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1904/1904.41)).
- **Step 6: Keep everything five years.** That covers the log, the 300A and the 301 incident reports ([29 CFR 1904.33](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1904/1904.33)).

## What happens if I miss the March 2 deadline?

OSHA is checking. Under a [March 2026 enforcement memo](https://www.osha.gov/memos/2026-03-23/injury-tracking-application-ita-non-responder-enforcement-program), OSHA compares open inspections each week against establishments that may not have submitted. Employment law firm Fisher Phillips reports that non-submitters can also land in the pool OSHA draws on for targeted inspections ([Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/did-your-company-miss-the-march-2-osha-electronic-recordkeeping-deadline)).

The maximum penalty for a posting violation is $16,550 ([OSHA](https://www.osha.gov/memos/2026-05-21/2026-annual-adjustments-osha-civil-penalties)). Small employers get a size reduction of up to 70% for companies with 11 to 25 employees. But the real cost is an inspection that looks at everything else in your plant.

## Why does this matter beyond paperwork?

Your log is the cheapest safety data you'll ever get. Three hand cuts at the same press tell you exactly where to look next. In my years running safety in manufacturing plants, the shops that kept an honest log usually found their next injury before it happened.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to post the 300A if we had zero injuries?

Yes. Enter zeros in each column total and post it from February 1 to April 30.

### Who can sign the OSHA 300A?

A company executive: an owner (sole proprietorship or partnership), a corporate officer, the top official at the site, or that person's direct supervisor.

### Does a 15-person shop have to submit the 300A online?

No. For manufacturers, online submission starts at 20 employees per establishment. A 15-person company still keeps the log and posts the 300A.

### Do temps and part-timers count toward 20 employees?

Yes. Part-time, seasonal and temporary workers all count for the electronic submission threshold.
